Privacy policy

Last updated: 25 October 2026

This privacy policy covers the Android app Zapli (package dev.anyapp.preview), distributed by SynapTech s. r. o. (“we”). For any privacy question, write to the developer email address shown on the app’s Google Play page.

In short: the app does not collect, send or share your data. Everything you keep in it stays on your phone. It goes online only to fetch the Zapli library’s tools and their updates from GitHub, and to download a tool from a link when you ask it to.

What the app does

Zapli installs and runs small apps (“tools”): the ones from the Zapli library, such as a to-do list, a water log, a blood pressure log, an expense tracker, loyalty cards and three games, and the ones you import: a file you pick, a file or text you open or share with it from another app, text you paste, or a link you give it. It reads only what you give it this way, and installs no new tool until you tap Install. It also checks the Zapli library for new tools and new versions: each time you open the app, at most once every 10 minutes, and when you tap Check now in the library’s Settings. A new version of a library tool you installed is applied by itself when it asks for nothing new; otherwise it waits for your review. The check is on from the start; you can turn the automatic check off in the library’s Settings, with Check for library updates.

Your data stays on your device

The tools and everything you enter in them are stored only in the app’s private storage on your phone. That can be sensitive data, such as your blood pressure readings, what you spend, your loyalty card numbers or your photos. When a tool asks for photos, you pick them in Android’s own picker, and the tool receives only the photos you choose: the app needs no permission to read your photos or other files. The tool keeps a copy that the app makes of each photo you take or pick, without your location or other metadata. We never receive any of it. The app uses the internet only for the library check and for links you import from. For the library check, it downloads the list of the library’s tools, signed by us, and the tools’ files from GitHub (raw.githubusercontent.com) over an encrypted connection. It sends none of your data, no cookies and nothing that identifies you or your phone; the request says only that it comes from Zapli. Like any website, GitHub sees your phone’s internet address (IP address), the time and which files are fetched, and handles them under GitHub’s privacy statement; the DNS service your phone uses sees the name raw.githubusercontent.com. When you import a tool from a link, the app downloads the file at that link once, over an encrypted connection and only after you confirm, from the website its confirm step names (for a link to a file’s page on GitHub, GitHub’s file host, raw.githubusercontent.com or gist.githubusercontent.com) and from any website that website sends the download on to. Each of them sees your phone’s internet address (IP address), the time, the link and that the request comes from Zapli, and handles them under its own terms and privacy policy; the DNS service your phone uses sees each of their names. The confirm step shows the whole link, which the website receives as it is. The app keeps only each website’s name, the time and how many bytes went each way, in a log on your phone for 90 days, never the link; it never downloads from that link again by itself, and the tool goes through its install review like a file you import. The tools themselves cannot connect to the internet: the app refuses every connection a tool asks for. Tools that contain their own code run on your phone in sandboxed processes of Android System WebView; for such a tool that you import, the app narrows how it could reach the internet but cannot fully stop it, and its install review says so. The app contains no advertising, analytics, tracking or crash-reporting code, and it shows no ads. Neither do the open-source libraries it keeps for such tools (listed under Library licences). Android’s automatic backup is turned off for this app, so its data is not copied to a cloud backup either.

Permissions

The app uses these Android permissions, each only for what is written here. It also has the Internet permission, which Android grants when you install the app, without asking, and uses it only for the library check and the downloads from links described above, never for a tool. With it comes Android’s permission to see whether the phone is online, which the app uses only to skip the check, or not to start a download, when it is not. Before a tool first uses the camera, shows notifications or sets exact alarms, the app asks you, and Android may ask as well. You can refuse, and later turn each of these off for one tool on its Permissions page in the app, or for the whole app in Android’s settings. Android grants Biometrics and Run at startup when you install the app, without asking:

  • Camera: to scan a barcode or QR code when you start a scan, and to take a photo when you tap to take one in a tool. A scan is analysed on the phone; its image is not stored or sent anywhere, and the tool receives only the decoded text. You take a photo in your phone’s own camera app; the tool then keeps a copy that the app makes of it on your phone, without your location or other metadata.
  • Notifications and Alarms & reminders: to show the reminders your tools set, on time.
  • Biometrics: to unlock a tool you protect with your fingerprint, face or screen lock. Android tells the app only whether unlocking succeeded; the app never receives biometric data.
  • Run at startup: to set your reminders again after the phone restarts.

When data leaves the app

Only when you choose it: when you share something through Android’s share menu, or save or export a file to a place you pick. It then goes to the app or service you chose, under its own terms; we are not involved and receive nothing.

Google Play

If you installed the app through Google Play, Google processes data under its own privacy policy. While we test the app through Google Play, we see in Google Play Console the email addresses we added to the testers list, and the statistics and crash reports that Google provides to developers, which come from devices whose owners agreed to share usage and diagnostics data with Google. We use them only to run the test and fix problems.

Retention and deletion

Data in the app stays on your phone until you delete it: uninstalling a tool in the app deletes its data, and uninstalling Zapli or clearing its storage in Android’s settings deletes everything. We hold no copy, so there is nothing for us to delete. We remove your email address from the testers list when the test ends or when you ask us.

Security

The data is kept in the app’s private storage, protected by Android’s app sandbox; other apps cannot read it. Tools are kept apart from each other, use only the phone features shown when you install them, and use the camera, notifications and alarms only after you allow it. The app takes a tool or an update from the library only when the list of tools carries our signature, which the app checks on your phone with a key built into it, and each file matches that list exactly; otherwise it keeps what it has. A tool downloaded from a link is checked on your phone like a file you import and installs only after you review it.

Children

The app is not directed at children.

Changes

If this policy changes, the app shows the new version here, and we publish it on our policy’s web page, each with a new date.
